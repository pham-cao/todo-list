Day navigation

Prototype on the paper notebook design, version 3

A clickable prototype for moving between days: turn one page back or forward, pick any date from a stamp, and go back to today with one tab. Every day works like today, so the owner can fill in yesterday or plan tomorrow. All interface text is Vietnamese, word for word from the requirements, and dark mode follows your system setting.

Start here

Open today and press the left arrow to reach yesterday, then the right arrow twice for tomorrow. Notice how the heading, the tape and Về hôm nay change. The flows and screen inventory are in flows.md, the new components in design-system.md (section "Day navigation"), and the review notes in critique.md.

Key design decisions

  • The navigation gets its own row under the title instead of the top right slot. The title, the arrows, the stamp and the tab do not fit on one line, and a squeezed title would wrap badly on phones.
  • Turning pages: two pen chevrons sit together as a pair. When the day changes, the lines slide in from the side you turned towards and the washi tape is pressed down again. This is the feature's one piece of motion, and reduced motion turns it off.
  • The date picker is a rubber date stamp around the native date input. At rest it shows only a calendar mark, because the native field prints the date in the browser's own order (often month first) and the date is already on the tape. With a keyboard, the stamp widens into the native field so the date can be typed.
  • Về hôm nay is an index tab that answers "am I on today?" at a glance. It is pen filled on any other day and a quiet dashed outline on today, while staying focusable (aria-disabled), as R5 asks.
  • Three more cues guard against adding a task to the wrong day: the heading drops "hôm nay", the tape names the day (Hôm qua, Ngày mai, or the plain date), and the placeholder says "ngày này".
  • Nothing else on the page changes. Rows, the star stamp, editing, deleting and the storage slip are the approved daily-task-log components, so Sửa, Xóa and the add errors link to those screens.
